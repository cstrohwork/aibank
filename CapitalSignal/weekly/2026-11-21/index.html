Weekly assessment

AI valuation risk became a broad capital-markets repricing. The S&P 500 declined 8.6% and the Nasdaq Composite 10.0% for November 16–20; the S&P 500 and Dow entered correction territory. The market's central question shifted from AI investment scale to prospective returns on capital, earnings resilience and funding costs. AP, Nov. 20; Reuters, Nov. 20.

Macro and cross-asset developments

Friday's decline was especially sharp: S&P 500 −3.70%, Nasdaq −5.28%, Dow −1.93%. Treasury demand rose as investors reduced equity risk; the 10-year yield was reported near 4.95% on Friday, down roughly 21 basis points from the prior week's approximately 5.15% level. Yield changes are not Treasury total returns. AP, Nov. 20; Reuters, Nov. 20.

Volatility surged as investors reconsidered AI spending, potential Federal Reserve tightening and financing conditions. Reuters, Nov. 20. A comparable VIX close-to-close weekly change was not independently verified and is intentionally excluded.

Corporate developments, IPOs and sector leadership

Technology: Nvidia fell approximately 14% on Friday and 23% for the week, among the largest losses as the market repriced AI-linked exposures. Semiconductor shares broadly declined. Reuters, Nov. 20.

IPO: Anthropic's public-market debut was followed by an approximately 9% Friday decline, highlighting sensitivity to valuation and secondary-market support. Reuters, Nov. 20. No unsupported weekly aggregate issuance total is presented.

Sector positioning: Technology was the main source of index weakness; defensive and value-oriented shares held up relatively better in Friday's rotation. This is a relative observation, not a claim that all defensive sectors gained over the week. Reuters, Nov. 20.

Implications for issuers and investors

IR teams should distinguish contracted AI revenue from speculative capacity demand, explain financing commitments and balance-sheet flexibility, and provide sensitivity to a higher cost of capital. A 13F report represents holdings on its reporting date, typically filed later; it cannot establish trading flows during November 16–20. Next week's watchpoints

Wednesday's GDP and personal-income/spending releases will test the growth and inflation narrative after a disorderly repricing. Thanksgiving's shortened trading week can amplify liquidity and execution risks. TD Economics, Nov. 20.
