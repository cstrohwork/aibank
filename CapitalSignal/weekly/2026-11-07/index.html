Market wrap · November 2–6

U.S. equities finished the November 2–6 trading week lower across all four major benchmarks. The Nasdaq Composite declined 3.4% and the S&P 500 lost 2.7%, while the Russell 2000 fell 2.1% and the Dow declined 0.9%. Friday brought a partial recovery in AI-related shares, but not enough to reverse the week's losses. Investors weighed the midterm election results, technology valuations and a stronger U.S. jobs report. AP

What you should know

Technology led the drawdown. The Nasdaq's weekly decline exceeded the S&P 500's by roughly 0.7 percentage point, illustrating pressure on growth-oriented equities. Volatility climbed. The VIX finished Friday at 25.64, versus 19.42 a week earlier, according to a market-close compilation. Rate expectations remain consequential. The 10-year Treasury yield ended near 4.86%, leaving discount-rate sensitivity central to equity valuation discussions. Market data

Market breadth & issuer implications

The equal-weight S&P 500 fell 1.06%, outperforming the capitalization-weighted index (−2.68%) by 1.62 percentage points. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.15% for the week. Market data.

NYSE new lows (352) exceeded new highs (237); AAII bears (48.4%) outnumbered bulls (34.1%). The breadth and sentiment backdrop is relevant for liquidity-sensitive issuers but does not itself demonstrate institutional sales. First Trust.

Capital markets: $125B Treasury refunding

On November 4, the Treasury announced $125 billion of 3-, 10- and 30-year issuance to refinance about $97 billion of privately held maturities and raise approximately $28 billion of new cash. The auctions comprise $58 billion in three-year notes on November 9, $42 billion in ten-year notes on November 10 and $25 billion in thirty-year bonds on November 12; settlement is November 16. U.S. Treasury.
